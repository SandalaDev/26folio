# Original Project Intent

> **Project:** sandala.dev
>
> This document captures the original vision for **sandala.dev**, the personal website and professional platform of **Abe Sandala**. It exists to preserve the intent behind the project before implementation details, design decisions, or future iterations influence its direction.
>
> The website is more than a portfolio. It is the public expression of my professional identity, my thinking, my engineering philosophy, and the consulting practice I am building. Every page, interaction, and technical decision should reinforce that vision.
>
> This document is the project's constitution. It defines _why_ the project exists, _who_ it serves, and _what principles_ should remain constant as the site evolves. Future contributors—human or AI—should treat this intent as authoritative and ensure their work remains faithful to it rather than rewriting or redefining its purpose.

---

# 1. What is this?

This project is the public home of my professional identity.

It is simultaneously a personal portfolio, an independent software consulting website, a lead generation platform, and a knowledge publication. Rather than presenting me as a freelancer who builds websites, it positions me as an independent software consultant and systems thinker who helps organizations solve meaningful business problems with thoughtfully engineered software.

The website should demonstrate technical excellence while communicating strategic thinking. Every page should reinforce that I understand both software engineering and the business outcomes software exists to achieve.

For business owners, the website should communicate trust, expertise and the long-term value of investing in custom software.

For employers and technical leaders, it should serve as evidence of engineering judgment, architectural thinking, product sense and implementation quality.

For developers and founders discovering my work through social media or search, it should become a destination for thoughtful writing, practical insights and technical exploration.

The site itself should be treated as a flagship product that showcases my capabilities through both its content and its implementation.

---

# 2. Who is it for?

This project intentionally serves multiple audiences without creating separate experiences for each.

## Primary Audience

Businesses seeking a long-term software partner.

This includes:

- Small and medium businesses
- Startups
- Entrepreneurs
- NGOs
- International companies
- Organizations expanding into African markets

Although I possess specialized expertise around African payment infrastructure and compliance (such as Mobile Money and ZRA Smart Invoice), the overall positioning should remain international. Visitors from anywhere in the world should feel that my services are relevant to them.

---

## Secondary Audience

Technical employers.

Including:

- CTOs
- Engineering Managers
- Recruiters
- Product companies
- Software agencies
- Remote-first companies

The site should communicate technical depth, product thinking, ownership and software craftsmanship rather than simply listing technologies.

---

## Tertiary Audience

Developers, designers and founders.

These visitors may discover my work through blog posts, technical articles, conference talks or social media. The website should encourage them to return regularly for thoughtful content and establish credibility beyond client work.

---

# 3. What does success look like?

This project succeeds if it consistently attracts high-quality opportunities rather than maximizing visitor numbers.

Success means:

- Qualified consulting inquiries from businesses that value long-term partnerships.
- Interest from international employers seeking senior-level engineering capability.
- Recognition as someone who combines technical excellence with strategic business thinking.
- Visitors leave understanding that I solve business problems rather than simply build websites.
- The website becomes a living demonstration of my design, engineering and product capabilities.
- The blog and essays establish an ongoing reputation for thoughtful technical writing.
- The site remains relevant for years by supporting the evolution of my consulting practice and professional career.

The quality of opportunities generated matters significantly more than the quantity.

---

# 4. Hard constraints

## Technology

Must be built using:

- Next.js
- Payload CMS
- TypeScript
- Tailwind CSS
- Modern component architecture
- Responsive-first design
- Excellent accessibility
- Strong SEO foundations

---

## Performance

The website itself should demonstrate engineering excellence.

Goals include:

- Excellent Core Web Vitals
- Fast global loading
- Minimal JavaScript where appropriate
- Progressive enhancement
- Thoughtful animations that never reduce usability

---

## Positioning

The site must never feel like:

- a generic freelancer portfolio
- a template agency website
- a corporate consultancy
- a personal blog with no commercial focus

Instead it should occupy the space between:

Personal Brand × Boutique Consultancy × Product Studio × Technical Publication

---

## Business Positioning

Websites are positioned as the beginning of a client's digital journey rather than the final deliverable.

The service hierarchy should naturally educate visitors toward increasingly valuable solutions including:

- Custom websites
- CMS implementation
- E-commerce
- Payment integrations
- Business systems
- Internal tools
- Automation
- AI integrations
- Long-term technical consulting

---

## Geographic Positioning

The website must appeal to international audiences.

African expertise is presented as a specialization rather than a limitation.

The messaging should feel equally relevant whether the visitor is from Zambia, Kenya, Germany, the UK or the United States.

---

# 5. Explicitly out of scope

This project is NOT intended to become:

- A generic web design agency.
- A marketplace for inexpensive websites.
- A template showcase.
- A collection of disconnected landing pages.
- A technology buzzword showcase.
- A personal diary.
- A developer résumé disguised as a website.
- A corporate enterprise consultancy pretending to have a large team.

The project should avoid chasing trends at the expense of timeless communication and thoughtful engineering.

---

# 6. Known risks & unknowns

## Risks

Balancing multiple audiences without confusing them.

Maintaining premium positioning while remaining approachable to smaller businesses.

Avoiding excessive technical jargon for business visitors.

Preventing the services section from becoming an overwhelming list of technologies.

Ensuring the About page remains authentic rather than self-promotional.

Maintaining consistency between personal storytelling, technical depth and commercial messaging.

---

## Unknowns

Final visual identity.

Brand photography.

Content publishing cadence.

Service packaging.

Future expansion into products, education or SaaS offerings.

The long-term consulting model may evolve, and the website should accommodate that evolution without requiring a complete repositioning.

# Guiding Principles

Every future design or engineering decision should reinforce these principles.

- Software exists to solve business problems.
- Simplicity is a competitive advantage.
- Engineering quality is visible through thoughtful execution.
- Education creates better clients than aggressive sales.
- Curiosity is part of the brand.
- Good software should age gracefully.
- Every project is an opportunity to teach.
- Long-term trust is more valuable than short-term conversions.
- Personal authenticity is a differentiator, not a weakness.
- The website should feel like meeting the person behind the work, not reading a brochure.
