# Curiosity

A collection of disciplines I keep returning to—not because I have to, but because I genuinely enjoy understanding how they work.

| Field | What keeps pulling me back |
|-------|-----------------------------|
| **Computer Systems** | The deeper you go, the more beautiful they become. From transistors to distributed systems, it's abstraction all the way up. |
| **Electronics** | We figured out how to manipulate invisible electrical signals into computers, satellites and smartphones. That's still insane to me. |
| **Mechanical Engineering** | If you forced me to pick one engineering discipline outside software, this is it. It's practical, elegant and useful almost everywhere. |
| **Electrical Engineering** | Everything is electrical. Understanding that isn't optional. |
| **Materials Science** | You can't build great things if you don't understand what they should be made of. |
| **Macroeconomics** | The closest thing we have to an owner's manual for the modern world. Incentives explain far more than people realize. |
| **Psychology** | Software is built for people. Understanding people is part of understanding software. |
| **Photography** | Light is weird. Cameras are weird. Somehow all that physics turns into emotion. I love that. |
| **Woodworking & Metal Fabrication** | There's something deeply satisfying about taking raw material and ending up with something useful that'll probably outlive you. |
| **Automotive Engineering** | The perfect intersection of engineering and emotion. Cars don't have to be loved, but somehow the good ones always are. |

# Principles

## Curiosity Compounds

Every new thing I learn makes something I already know more useful. The connections aren't always obvious at first, but they show up eventually.

---

## Learn Systems, Not Recipes

I don't like memorizing steps without understanding why they work. Once I understand the system, learning the tools becomes much easier.

---

## Fundamentals Age Slowly

Tools come and go. Fundamentals stick around.

---

## Everything Teaches Something

I don't separate my interests into useful and useless. Photography, economics, engineering and music all influence how I think and how I build software.

---

## Good Design Removes Effort

The best design isn't the one that gets noticed. It's the one that makes things feel obvious.

---

## Complexity Should Be Earned

If something is complicated there should be a very good reason for it.

---

## Ownership Matters

People should own the software they depend on.

---

## Build For The Long Term

I'd rather build something that still makes sense ten years from now than something impressive for five minutes.

---

## Taste Is A Skill

Knowing what to leave out is just as important as knowing what to put in.

---

## Truth Is The Best Default

If I don't know something I'd rather say I don't know than pretend I do.

---

## Fairness

If I wouldn't accept it being done to me, I shouldn't do it to anyone else.

---

## Sleep Well

Conscience and intuition exist for a reason. I'd rather lose and sleep well than win and have to justify something I knew wasn't right.

# Sources of Inspiration

## Non-fiction

### How to Speak Money

Language is often the gatekeeper to a field. Learning the language of finance opened an entirely new way of understanding the economy.

### Capital in the Twenty-First Century

Capital and labour create wealth in fundamentally different ways.

### Basic Economics

Prices are signals. There are no solutions, only trade-offs.

### The New Human Rights Movement

A compelling argument that our economic system should ultimately be judged by how well it serves people.

### Flow

Happiness isn't found. It's engineered.

### Atomic Habits

Life outcomes are simply habits compounded over time.

### Deep Work

Deep focus is becoming increasingly rare—and increasingly valuable.

### Steve Jobs

Love him or hate him, Steve Jobs was the OG cult-of-personality tech CEO. Every Silicon Valley founder since has tried to copy the act. The difference is Steve wasn't acting. He was about that life. Today we get kabuki theatre on podcasts, conference stages and Twitter.

### The Almanack of Naval Ravikant

Happiness isn't a reward waiting at the finish line. You either know how to be happy now or you'll find a reason not to be happy later too.

### The Virtues of War

When it's time to think, think. When it's time to decide, decide. But once it's go time... it's go time, baby.

## Fiction

### A Canticle for Leibowitz

The first truly great piece of fiction I ever read. Completely blindsided me because I still don't think I like dystopian fiction.

### The Expanse

I practically lived in this universe. Audiobooks during 1,200 km road trips. Kindle until I couldn't keep my eyes open. I've never been sadder finishing a series.

### A Song of Ice and Fire

I only picked these up because of the TV show. Thank God I did. George's world-building is on another level. Despite the attention these books get, I still think they're underrated.

### Ancillary Justice

My favorite work of fiction. I've read it almost every year and still can't properly explain why I love it.
